Hand painted / counter fascia / nine plates

Fascia

A coffee bar paints its name once and then spends years adding to it. This wall is told in four windows, each one laid out differently, so the eye never settles into a single rhythm. Lime runs across the plaster in long diagonals and never turns back.

I

The grinder

Two plates, one wide and one narrow, the way a wall looks before anybody has touched it again. The wide plate bleeds past the right edge of the page; the narrow one holds the left margin and carries the whole window on its own.

II

The shelf

Three plates on one line with a great deal of nothing between them. Nothing is centred here: the first plate is pushed hard against the left margin, the last one is cut by the right edge, and the middle one floats.

III

The corner table

Two plates stacked the other way round, so this window reads downwards: a narrow one held high against the right margin and a wider one dropping underneath it. The gap between them is the largest on the wall.

IV

Closing time

The last window, and the only one that is short. Two plates on one line, both cropped hard at the bottom edge of the page, with a single hairline rule running away to the right. The wall ends here and the lime keeps going.
