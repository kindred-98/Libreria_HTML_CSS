01 Sketch

A shelf for
the loose work

Atelier keeps six creative tools on one floating shelf. Move the pointer along it and the icons rise to meet you; the neighbours grow with a falloff, the contact shadow spreads, and the active tool keeps a slow ring pulsing beneath it.

Sketch is where everything starts: a pressure-aware pencil, a charcoal that respects the grain of the virtual paper, and an eraser that leaves a ghost rather than a hole. Pages stack into notebooks, notebooks stack into projects, and nothing is ever silently discarded.

  • EngineVector rasterised at 4× and cached
  • Brushes42 presets · 6 pressure curves
  • UndoUnlimited · stored per page
  • ExportPage, spread or whole notebook

02 Pixels

The raster tool that stays out of trouble

Pixels handles photographic work: non-destructive layers, a curve editor that shows the histogram underneath it, and a healing brush that samples from the frame you are actually looking at rather than the one the algorithm prefers.

Every adjustment lives in a stack you can fold flat and reopen a year later. The file remembers which monitor it was graded on, because colour arguments are much shorter when the evidence is inside the document.

  • Depth16-bit working space · 32-bit optional
  • LayersUnlimited · grouped and named
  • ProfilesEmbedded and reported on open
  • HistorySnapshots at every export

03 Vectors

Curves you can argue with

Vectors draws with four-handle curves, live booleans and a grid that snaps geometry without snapping the eye. Outlines can be drawn, measured and corrected in the same breath, which is the only workflow that survives contact with a sign shop.

Type converts to outlines only when you ask it to. Until then, changing the weight of a headline re-flows the whole composition instead of quietly breaking it into shapes nobody can edit next Tuesday.

  • HandlesFour per point · mirrored on demand
  • BooleansUnion, subtract, intersect, exclude
  • StrokeVariable width · pressure from tablet
  • OutputOptimised paths · no stray decimals

04 Frames

Twelve frames a second, on purpose

Frames is the animation bench: onion skin, a dope sheet that fits on one screen, and export presets for the three formats anybody actually asks for. It defaults to twelve frames a second because hand-drawn motion looks better when it is not pretending to be video.

Keyframes interpolate with an editable curve rather than a preset ease, so a bounce can be tuned by feel instead of by trial. The timeline ripples under the playhead while scrubbing, which makes rough cuts feel like film running through a gate.

  • Rate8 to 60 frames · 12 default
  • Onion skin3 before · 3 after · tinted
  • LayersPer frame or across the sequence
  • ExportSequence, loop or sheet

05 Sounds

A small room for loud ideas

Sounds records, trims and layers audio with a mixer that shows you three things only: level, position and what is clipping. Everything else lives one screen deeper, waiting for the moment it becomes useful rather than decorating the first one.

Samples come from a library of room tones recorded in the studio itself, so a transition can sit in the same acoustic space as the dialogue it follows. Licensed material is marked in the bin and never silently replaced.

  • Mixer8 buses · true peak metering
  • Record24-bit · 48 kilohertz
  • EffectsGate, delay, convolution reverb
  • Library190 room tones · labelled by city

06 Vault

Where the finished things live

Vault holds the assets the other five tools agree on: palettes, type ramps, brush files, exported frames and the licences that travel with them. Anything placed in a document is checked against the vault on open, and a missing licence raises a warning before the file leaves the machine.

Two copies stay local, one stays on the shelf drive, and a snapshot is written every Sunday whether or not the week was busy. Restore is a single drag, and the vault keeps a readable log of who changed what and when.

  • CopiesTwo local · one offsite
  • SnapshotSundays at 03:00 · kept 12 weeks
  • LicencesChecked on every open
  • SearchFilename, colour, project or date
