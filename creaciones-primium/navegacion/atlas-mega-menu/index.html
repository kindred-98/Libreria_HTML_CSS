Three columns, three climates

Open Destinations and the panel spreads across the page: links on the left with their own bullet facts, a scene on the right. Move down the columns with the arrow keys and the scene dissolves from sand to sea to stone.

Route 01 · Deserts

Dunes of Meridian

Four days on foot across a ridge sea of sand, walking the crests at first light when the wind has stopped and the shadow of each dune still lies across the next one.

Camps are made on the leeward side where the sand is packed hard, and the cook travels ahead by truck along the hardpan to meet the party each evening.

Route 02 · Deserts

Salt flat crossing

A two day traverse of the white pan, timed so that the surface holds its crust in the cool hours and the horizon stays a single unbroken line in every direction.

Water is carried for the whole crossing. The polygon seams are photographed each season, because they shift by a few metres after every flood.

Route 03 · Coastlines

Fjord light

Summer nights that never go fully dark, spent on the water with a small boat and a keeper who reads the weather off the colour of the far wall.

The route links three lighthouse stations by ferry, each with a room above the lamp room and a logbook that visiting travellers are expected to sign.

Route 04 · Coastlines

Cliff path

Eighteen kilometres of coastal footpath cut into slate, rising and falling with every headland and dropping to coves where the tide has worn steps of its own.

In autumn the path is walked west to east so the wind stays behind you, and the ferry at the end runs twice a day whatever the timetable claims.

Route 05 · Highlands

Terrace valleys

Stepped fields carved a thousand years ago, still farmed by hand, with mule tracks that switch back between walls of dry stone and channels of cold water.

Three nights are spent in stone huts above the fields, where the harvest is measured in sacks and the price of grain is still argued over at the door.

Route 06 · Highlands

Glacier pass

A single long day over the pass while the snow is still firm, roped together for the short traverse and off the ice by early afternoon when the sun softens it.

The descent follows a moraine of loose grey stone to a hut with a wood stove, which is the reason most people choose this side of the mountain.

Appendix A

Field notes

Every route is walked once by the person who draws it. Sketches, ferry timetables and the price of bread in each village go into the same notebook and come back out as the map you carry.
