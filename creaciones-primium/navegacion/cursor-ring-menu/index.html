Six tools, one circle

The wheel keeps every brush within a wrist of the cursor. Tab into it and the arrow keys walk the ring one slot at a time: the option under the cursor grows, the guide arc swings to meet it, and the hub reads out the tool you are about to pick.

Tool 01 · Vector

Pen nib

Every click lays an anchor and every drag pulls its handles, so a curve can be corrected long after the stroke was made. The nib snaps to the grid when you hold the modifier, which is how a logo outline stays honest.

The preview line shows the segment before it is committed, and a closed path fills itself the moment the last anchor meets the first.

Tool 02 · Dry media

Pencil

Pressure drives both width and opacity, and a tilt of the stylus turns the stroke on its side for shading. The grain of the virtual paper shows through lightly, so a soft pencil never looks like ink.

Four grades are stored in the wheel: hard for construction lines, soft for tone, and a coloured pair for the marks you intend to erase later.

Tool 03 · Wet media

Brush

Bristle count, loading and drying are modelled separately, which is why the first pass of a stroke is rich and the fourth one is nearly dry. Speed keeps the edge sharp; hesitation lets the pigment pool.

Mixing happens on a separate layer that can be flattened once the colour is right, keeping the paintable layer untouched underneath.

Tool 04 · Correction

Eraser

The eraser works on the mask rather than the pixels, so nothing is lost until the file is flattened. Hard edges cut clean shapes; soft edges feather into the tone the way a kneaded rubber does on paper.

Used with the modifier held down it flips to restore, which brings back the last section you removed without touching anything around it.

Tool 05 · Flat colour

Fill

Tolerance decides how far the colour travels from the pixel you touched, and gap closing bridges the small holes a sketch always leaves behind. The preview floods the area before you release, so the choice can be judged in place.

Fills land on their own layer by default, ready to be tinted or masked without disturbing the line work.

Tool 06 · Measure

Ruler and guides

Drag from the edge strip to pull out a guide, then hold the tool against it and the stroke snaps to the straight edge with a light tick. Angles lock to the marks engraved on the blade: fifteen, thirty, forty-five.

The readout stays on screen in tabular figures, so two measurements can be compared without writing anything down.

Appendix

Keyboard shortcuts

The wheel answers to the arrow keys in either direction and wraps around at the end, so the tool opposite your current one is always three presses away. Home returns to the pen, End lands on the ruler.

Enter follows the focused slot into its note below, Escape puts focus back on slot 01, and the guide arc resets with it. Nothing on the wheel needs a pointer to be reached.
