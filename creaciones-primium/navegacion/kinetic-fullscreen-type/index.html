01 / Programme

Seven nights, forty-one films, no galas

The programme is built around three strands that run every night: a restored print at twenty-one thirty, a new voice at half past twenty-two, and a midnight slot given entirely to shorts under twelve minutes. Nothing overlaps, so a ticket for one screening is a ticket for the whole evening in that room.

Every film is introduced from the stage by someone who worked on it, and every introduction is capped at four minutes. The full grid, with running times and print formats, is printed nightly on a single sheet at the door in black on cream.

02 / Venues

One theatre, one salon, one open patio

The Teatro Marés holds four hundred and twenty seats under a proscenium that has not been repainted since 1961, and it carries the restored prints. Two streets away the Sala Lumen is a former electrical substation with ninety chairs and the best acoustics in the city.

The third venue is a courtyard between the rope factory buildings, open to the sky and free to enter after eleven at once. When it rains the patio screening moves into the loading bay, projector and all, and the audience sits on the bales that are still there.

03 / Guests

Directors who stay for the whole week

Six filmmakers are in residence for the full edition, which means the morning conversations are not press junket questions. They watch each other's films at nine in the morning in an empty theatre, then argue about the projection until the doors open.

The residency covers travel, a room above the salon and one printing plate per guest. In exchange each director selects a film from the archive that the festival has never shown, and stands up to introduce it themselves.

04 / Tickets

Three passes and a single nightly ticket

A night ticket covers everything screening in one venue on one evening, including the short-film slot after midnight. The week pass opens all three venues for all seven nights and holds a reserved seat in the theatre until fifteen minutes before the house opens.

Students, residents of Porto Velo and anyone out of work pay the concession rate without proving anything at all. Under sixteens enter every patio screening free, and the box office keeps ten seats for each show for people who simply turn up.

05 / Archive

Twenty-seven editions kept on tape and paper

Since the first edition in a rope store with a borrowed projector, the festival has kept every programme, every introduction and every audience survey in a steel cabinet above the salon. Digitisation finished in 2024, but the paper originals are still the version cited in research.

The archive room opens on weekday afternoons with a reading lamp and a stool. Visitors can request any edition since 1998, and the attendant will bring the box while a small reel of that year's ident plays on a monitor in the corner.
