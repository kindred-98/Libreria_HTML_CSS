01 / Accounts

Every balance on one stack of cards

The first tab holds the whole relationship at a glance: the everyday account that salary lands in, a vault that rounds every purchase up to the next euro, and the joint account you share with the person you moved in with. Nothing opens in a submenu.

Balances refresh the moment the ledger changes rather than on a timer, so the figure on screen is the figure the merchant already saw. Pending card holds are shown as a separate line and drop off within three working days.

02 / Payments

Sent in two taps, recalled in one

Transfers to another Cobalto account settle in under a second at any hour, including on a Sunday. Everything else uses the same rail the bank uses internally, which means a payment made at 23:50 arrives at 07:02 rather than three days later.

Scheduled payments can be paused from the same row that created them, and a transfer sent to the wrong reference can be recalled for fourteen minutes — long enough to fix the mistake you only notice after you put the phone down.

03 / Cards

Freeze it from the queue at the till

The card tab shows the physical card, its expiry and the three controls that actually matter: freeze, contactless off, and a monthly ceiling that applies to the card rather than to the account. Freezing takes effect before the confirmation animation finishes.

Virtual cards are created with a merchant name already stamped on them and expire ninety days after the last charge, so a subscription you cancelled cannot quietly renew. Every card carries its own spending limit, independent of the balance behind it.

04 / Insights

Where the month actually went

Insights groups the ledger by merchant rather than by category code, so the coffee you buy four times a week appears once, with a total, instead of hiding inside a bar labelled food. The comparison is always against your own last six months.

Weekly totals arrive on Sunday evening with one number and one sentence. If spending on a single merchant doubles, the app says so in plain language and offers to set a ceiling on that merchant alone.

05 / Alerts

Three notifications, none of them noise

Alerts fire for money in, money out over your chosen threshold, and anything that looks like a card used far from your phone. Marketing never appears in this tab, which is why the badge on the bar can be trusted to mean something.

Clearing the counter marks everything as read on this device and on the web session, without dismissing a single notification from your lock screen. You can restore the example count below to see the badge fill up again.
