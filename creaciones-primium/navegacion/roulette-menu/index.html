The only rule at this table

The number lit on the layout is the chapter you are reading, and the wheel turns until that house is under the ball. The ball itself never moves; on a real wheel it is thrown once and the wheel is turned until the two meet.

The wheel only ever turns forwards, so going back a chapter costs a full turn rather than unwinding. A wheel that could be spun either way would be a dial, and a dial is not a wheel.

The gold track round the rim is where the ball runs. It is empty most of the time and it is the only part of the mechanism that is meant to be looked at rather than watched.

Number 1 · the table, then the wheel

The Wheel and the Table

A roulette wheel is a bowl with a tilted floor, a ridge across the middle and a metal track around the inside of the rim. The ball is thrown into the bowl, it runs down the slope, it climbs the opposite side, and it settles into the track, and the dealer then turns the bowl until the ball and the winning pocket meet. Nothing about that sequence is a secret and everything about it is a machine from the nineteenth century that has not been redesigned since.

The table comes first in every casino and the wheel is the piece people look at. The table is where the money is, the table is printed, the table is where a game of roulette is actually played, and the wheel is the machine that decides which way the money goes. The layout under this wheel is the whole of it in miniature: six numbers, one lit, and a wheel that has to come round to meet them.

Number 2 · red and black, and the green that is neither

Rouge et Noir

The even money bet is the oldest bet on the table and the worst of them, and the reason is a single zero. On a wheel with thirty seven pockets, eighteen are red, eighteen are black, and one is green, and a bet on a colour wins just under half the time. The house does not need skill, it needs the arithmetic of a wheel that does not divide evenly by the things being bet on.

Which is why a roulette table is a machine for turning a pleasure into a percentage. The green zero exists so that a bet on a colour loses one time in thirty seven, and the effect of that on a large number of small bets is a profit of about five percent of everything that goes through it, every hour, for as long as the wheel is turning.

Number 3 · two moving parts that never touch

The Ball Stops

The ball is thrown, the wheel is turned, and the two never come into contact. This surprises people who have only seen a wheel on television, where the ball appears to be captured by a pocket. It is not: the ball runs on the outer track, the pockets are on the inner cone, and the result is a comparison between two independent motions, which is the entire mechanical trick of the game.

Because the ball is heavier than the plastic it is made of and the track is polished to a very high standard, the ball slows down over about eight seconds and comes to rest with a sound like a small coin dropped on a table. The header above works the same way round. The wheel turns under the ball, the ball does not move, and the stopping is abrupt: the wheel is decelerating for a second and then it is simply not turning.

Number 4 · a name that travelled and lost its meaning

Le Trefle, the Clover

The French name for a roulette bet on three numbers is le trefle, the clover, and the word survives in every casino in the world in English as a street bet, which is a name that describes where the numbers are printed and nothing at all about what the bet is. The three numbers are the last of the low second dozen, and the bet pays eleven to one because there are eleven ways on the layout that are not it.

The whole layout is a set of names for shapes. A street is three numbers in a line. A corner is four numbers meeting at a corner. A six line is six numbers in two rows. A column is a third of the layout and pays the same as a dozen because it is a dozen. Nobody named any of it after a picture, and the pictures are the part that survived.

Number 5 · what the layout is really worth

The House Edge

Every bet on a roulette layout is priced so that the sum of all the payouts is slightly less than the amount staked. A straight up bet pays thirty five to one on a wheel with a single zero, which is a bet that wins one time in thirty six. A dozen pays two to one on twelve numbers out of thirty six. Put them together and the arithmetic of the layout does not work in the player's favour by any amount that matters.

The number on the header is the only number that moves on this page, and it moves because the chapter moved. The edge on the table does not move at all, and it is the same on every number, which is a useful thing to remember: a roulette wheel is not a game of picking the right number, it is a game of paying for the privilege of picking a number at all.

Number 6 · unrolled, a wheel is a number line

A Wheel Is a Number Line

If you take a wheel apart and lay the cone out flat, what you have is a number line with a zero on it, and the order of the numbers around the rim is not the order of the numbers on the table. The number sequence was designed so that no two consecutive numbers sit next to each other on the layout, which spreads the bets out, and the order around the rim was designed so that no two consecutive numbers are adjacent to each other in the wheel either, which spreads the pockets out.

That is the last thing there is to say about a wheel, and it is the reason the wheel on this page has six houses and not thirty seven. Six houses unrolled is six chapters, and a wheel that stops under a ball is a machine for answering one question: which of the things on the table are you in. The ball does not move, the wheel does, and the number lit on the layout is the answer.
