How to read the diagram

Line 1 runs the length of the city, from the carriage sheds in the west to the harbour wall.

Line 2 is the ridge line. It stays deep, which is why its headway is wider.

The cream connector is not a service. It is the cross passage that lets you change lines at either interchange.

A ringed platform is an interchange: change here and keep the same ticket.

Stop 01 · Line 1 · 0 m

Depot Halt

The line begins where the city begins to move things. Behind the low sheds of the carriage depot, six hundred metres of running tunnel end in a buffer stop that a night gang has quietly repainted twice a decade. Depot Halt is not a destination so much as a rumour: the platform is open, the lamps are on, and the trains that leave here spend the whole day somewhere more interesting.

Services start at four in the morning, spaced seven minutes apart, and the first one pulls out empty. By the time the second pulls out, the first is already under the river wall with nobody aboard except a driver who has made the same six stops so many times that she sets her watch by the dip of the tunnel.

Stop 02 · Interchange · 11 m

Market Cross

Everything converges here. The northbound and southbound platforms sit one above the other, and between them a cross passage climbs to the cream connector, the narrow stair that the original engineer described in his notebook as the shortest walk he could find between the two deep levels. Four minutes, if you do not stop to buy anything, which nobody does.

The market above keeps its own timetable. Stalls come down at nine, the arcade fills at eleven, and the escalator bank is the only place in Vallmar where a crowd will queue without being asked. The station concourse was widened twice and still finds a way to be narrow, because a market is a moving thing and no architect has ever won that argument.

Stop 03 · Line 1 · 9 m

Quayside

The tunnel opens out here into a vaulted chamber cut straight into the harbour wall, and for eight months of the year you can taste the salt. Cargo handlers use the same entrances as commuters and wear the same yellow badges, so the morning platform is a small port with a roof.

Trains do not run past Quayside; they stop, wait for a signal that is almost always green, and go back. It is the eastern end of Line 1 and the only station on the diagram with a footbridge that leads nowhere, kept in place because the harbour authority once promised a second quay.

Stop 04 · Line 2 · 148 m

Granite Hill

Line 2 drops through the granite and does not come up for four kilometres. The bore is single track in places and the wall is bare, sweating in summer, streaming in February. Staff walk the whole section with a lamp before the first service because the rock moves, and the railway keeps a book of every crack it has ever measured.

Granite Hill station is a single island platform with a ceiling so low that the carriage lights seem to brush it. Half the people waiting here are going down, not up. The ventilation shaft behind the bench has a grille painted with a small blue ring, and it makes a noise all night that passengers have learned to sleep through.

Stop 05 · Interchange · 152 m

Quarry Gate

The ridge line surfaces for ninety metres, in the notch where the old granite quarry used to load onto wagons, and it is the only place on the network where daylight reaches a Line 2 platform. The gate in the name is still there: a folding iron barrier kept in working order because the quarry is still leased and still occasionally explodes rock at six in the morning.

Change here for the cream connector and the long climb back to the shallow line. Three minutes, and the light changes completely: down into the bore, the walls close in and the noise of the fans arrives before the train does.

Stop 06 · Line 2 · 61 m

Workshops

The eastern terminus, built on the floor of a disused rolling stock works that kept its roof and lost everything else. The platforms sit between the old inspection pits, and the whole place smells of hot oil and cold stone. Trains arrive, empty, and do not go out again until the morning.

There is a signal box at the end of platform 2 with a view along nine hundred metres of straight tunnel, which is the longest sightline on the network and the reason engineers stand there on the first of every month. Beyond it, the line stops. Past the stop, the tunnel narrows and the map simply stops as well.
