01 Archive

Everything kept twice

The documentary archive holds four thousand two hundred reels of film, tape and paper, catalogued twice: once on cards in the cabinet, once in the register, and never for long in only one of them.

Material arrives from broadcasters, families, harbour offices and one solicitor who still posts reels in bubble wrap. Nothing is thrown away. Reels that cannot be played are stored in the cold room until a machine exists that can read them without eating them.

The reading room seats eight, the projector is a 1968 model kept alive by a retired engineer, and the rule about cotton gloves applies to everybody including the directors.

02 Collections

Four shelves, four habits

The holdings are split by what the material was made for rather than by who made it: work, news, family and the state. A wedding filmed by a professional sits with the families; the same wedding cut into a news item sits with the broadcasters.

This is an unpopular arrangement with cataloguers and a very popular one with readers, who remember what a reel was for long before they remember who shot it.

  • Work

    Labour and harbour

    Eleven hundred reels of shift changes, crane operations, strike meetings and the last day of the rope factory.

  • News

    Broadcast rushes

    Unedited material from two regional stations, including the outtakes that were never allowed to air.

  • Family

    Home movies

    Nine hundred reels donated since 1994, mostly silent, mostly summer, occasionally extraordinary.

  • State

    Survey and record

    Coastal surveys, road schemes and the filming that accompanied every public works contract issued between 1960 and 1985.

03 Coastal survey

Seven years of shoreline

Between 1972 and 1979 the coastal survey filmed the same forty-two kilometres of shore twice a year, at low water, with the same tripod and the same three lenses. The purpose was engineering: to measure how fast the breakwaters were going.

The accidental result is the finest portrait of the coast the region owns. Gritty, unmoving, patient, and full of people who did not know they were being recorded because the crew had long since stopped asking them to move.

04 Reel 042

The harbour at dawn

Reel 042 was shot on the fourteenth of May 1974, starting at ten past five, and it is the only reel in the survey where the crew clearly gave up on the breakwater and started filming the men instead.

Fourteen minutes, no titles, no narration. The camera holds on the net menders for two full minutes, which by the standards of a survey film is an act of open rebellion, and it is the reason this reel is the one shown to every student who visits.
