Sheet 01 · headline

Turn three in one paragraph

Revenue rose nine per cent against the same quarter last year, driven almost entirely by two contracts signed in the spring and by a repair business the group bought for almost nothing. Operating margin fell by a point and a half, and the board has said publicly that this was the plan.

The plan was to spend the first two quarters buying time and the third quarter paying for it. Cash at the bank is lower than it was in January, and the bridge facility that funded the repair acquisition is drawn to just under a fifth of its limit.

Sheet 02 · revenue

Where the nine per cent came from

Two thirds of the growth sits in a single line: the maintenance contracts signed with the two port authorities in April. They are long, they are quiet, and they will still be there next year. The remaining third is split almost evenly between the repair acquisition and a general improvement in the domestic business.

Nothing in the quarter came from a one-off sale of equipment, which the group has deliberately stopped doing. A quarter that ends with a gain on disposal is a quarter whose next quarter has to be explained.

Sheet 03 · margins

The cost of buying time

Margin fell because the repair business was integrated faster than it was scaled, and integration costs are front loaded while the savings arrive over four quarters. The engineering headcount in the repair division is now at the number the model assumed, and the model is being run monthly rather than quarterly.

Two things are not going the way the model assumed: the price of the steel that goes into the larger repair jobs, and the amount of overtime the new site has needed. Together they are worth a point and a quarter of margin, which is most of the fall.

Sheet 04 · regions

Three regions, three stories

The northern region is the one to watch. It grew eleven per cent, it holds both port contracts, and it is the only region where the group has been able to hire ahead of demand without paying for it later. The southern region shrank three per cent on the closure of a small site that was losing money for three years.

Export is flat, which after two difficult years is being described in the board minutes as stability. The minutes also note that stability in a shrinking market is not growth, and that the fourth quarter must be handled with the same discipline as the third.

Sheet 05 · outlook

What the fourth quarter is expected to do

The guidance has not moved: low single digit revenue growth, margin a little under the third quarter, and no further acquisitions. The bridge facility is expected to be repaid in part from operating cash before the year end, which would leave the group with a facility it does not need and an unusually clean balance sheet.

Two risks are named in the report rather than hidden in the wording. Steel remains volatile, and the southern closure removes capacity that the northern region will have to cover in the winter peak. Neither is a reason to change the plan; both are reasons to watch it closely.

Sheet 06 · appendix

Figures, definitions and the small print

Operating profit is stated before the amortisation of acquired intangibles and before any exceptional item, which is the same basis used for the comparative quarters. Where a figure has been restated, the appendix says so and gives the original as well, because a restatement without the original is a correction with no memory.
