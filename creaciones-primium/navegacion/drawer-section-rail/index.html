01 / Sessions

Two-hour blocks, one crew, no rush

A session at Halide is booked in two-hour blocks with a technician already in the room: stands struck, flags up and the first test strip hanging before you arrive. Studio A keeps a permanent four-metre seamless roll, while Studio B is left empty for furniture and larger builds.

Cancellations move the block rather than losing it, provided the studio hears before noon on the previous working day. Every booking ends with a card of captures written to the house drive and a note of what the light was doing.

02 / Lighting floor

Twenty-two heads and a wall of modifiers

Stage B carries the house lighting: four strobe heads on ceiling rails, two on floor stands and a battery kit that leaves the building for location work. The modifier wall is arranged by throw distance rather than by brand, so a search for a hard six-foot source ends at the same hook every time.

Continuous tungsten lives behind the black curtain for motion work, with scrims and a dimmer bank rated far above anything the room actually draws. Nothing is locked off; every stand is weighted and every cable is matted.

03 / Darkroom

Chemistry held at twenty degrees

The wet lab runs three enlargers and two washing tanks behind a light trap. Developer, stop and fix are mixed fresh each week and checked with a thermometer before the first sheet goes in, because a half degree of drift is visible in the shadows of a fibre print.

The amber safelight is the only lamp in the room, and it is the lamp you can see breathing on the rail outside. Prints are dried flat between blotters overnight, then pressed under glass for a day before they are trimmed.

04 / Print lab

Archival sheets, selenium toned

The print lab turns digital negatives and film alike into sheets that outlive everyone in the building: fibre-based paper, selenium toner for the cold blacks and a wash that removes residual hypo down to the last trace. Each sheet is numbered on the verso with the negative, the grade and the date.

Proof sets are printed small and fast so decisions are made on paper rather than on a screen, then the chosen frame is enlarged to size and printed twice — one for the client, one for the archive drawer.

05 / Gallery wall

Front room, six frames at a time

The front room hangs six frames at once, always from work made inside the building, and changes on the first Thursday of every month. Visitors walk in from the street without an appointment; the wall is lit with two narrow-beam spots and nothing else.

Every hanging carries a small card with the process, the paper and the year — the same three facts printed on the back of each sheet. Sales are recorded in a ledger rather than a till, in keeping with the age of the room.

06 / Rates

Plain numbers on a plain card

Studio time is charged by the block, the technician by the hour and the darkroom by the session. There is no membership and no minimum spend, and the rate card has not changed since the paper prices did in January.

Students and long-form documentary work pay half, by request and without paperwork. Everything quoted includes chemistry, test strips and the archive drawer that keeps the negatives safe after the job is finished.
