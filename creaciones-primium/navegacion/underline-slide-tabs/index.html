Section 01 · Before the ram moves

Inspection

Every shift starts with the machine isolated. Throw the main isolator to off, pull the control pendant plug, and put a personal padlock on the isolator before anything is opened. The press holds about four hundred kilos of stored force at full stroke, and the safety valve is not a lock.

With the pendant still unplugged, wipe the ram seal face and run the bed through its full travel by hand. Listen for the drag that means the gibs have lost adjustment, and check that the two alignment pins drop into the bed holes without force. The frame takes a set over the first few hundred strokes and the shims are meant to take up that set, not the pins.

310 Nm
Ram parallelism, top to bottom
0.4 mm max
Hydraulic level, sight glass

Section 02 · Calendar and consumables

Scheduled service

Oil changes follow hours, not calendar: fifty every 1 000 ram hours and the return filter element every 500. Write the running hours on the service plate with a paint pen, because the hour meter on these machines drifted and a set of rebuilds was done far too early on a press whose meter was optimistic.

The monthly list is short and should stay that way. Grease the two gib adjusters, check the pedal guard return spring, look at the ram seal for the flat shine that means a cut, and run the pendant lead through the drag chain to find a chafe mark before it opens a conductor. Anything else belongs to the annual strip.

Section 03 · Relief valve and squareness

Pressure and alignment

Set the relief valve with a calibrated gauge in the test port, never against the machine gauge. The working range is 120 to 160 bar for general pressing and the gauge needle should be steady at each step, not hunting. A needle that drops and recovers on every stroke means air in the cylinder, and the fix is to bleed at the highest point with the ram stopped.

Alignment is corrected with shims, never by forcing the pins. Loosen the bed bolts, slide a feeler gauge under the low corner, and add shim until the gap under the ram plate reads even from front to back. Recheck at full extension, because a frame that is square at the bottom is often twisted at the top.

120 – 160 bar
Shim stock, bed
0.05 / 0.10 mm

Section 04 · Pendant and control

Fault codes

The pendant lists six codes and nothing else, so the fault has to be read from what the machine is doing rather than from a screen. Write down the code, the oil temperature and the position of the ram when it appeared, then work down the table; two thirds of the calls turn out to be a guard interlock out of adjustment.

Code E4 in particular is worth remembering. It is raised when the two-hand control sees both buttons within a fifth of a second of each other, which normally means one button has a worn contact and the machine is being run one handed. That is a stop-work fault, not a fault code to be cleared.

Before you leave the machine

Safety notes

Lower the ram onto the bolster blocks, never onto a part. Prop the pendant on its hook, put the isolator padlock back on, and leave the two hand buttons clear so the next shift cannot start the press without noticing the padlock.

Hydraulic fluid on a hot ram is a burn risk, and a burst line at 160 bar is a whip hazard that the standard guard does not stop. Clean the frame before you leave it: the slip that puts somebody on the floor is usually a puddle somebody else left.
