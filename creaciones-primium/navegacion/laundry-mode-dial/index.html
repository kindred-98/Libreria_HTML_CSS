Programme 01 · Presoak

Soak

Thirty degrees of water with no movement at all: the garment sits while the bath does the work. Used for tannin stains and for pieces whose dye has never been tested, the soak is the only programme that never touches the fibre.

Programme 02 · Hand wash

Hand

Four slow turns a minute, the way a hand would press a piece under the surface and lift it again. The drum stops between rotations so nothing is stretched while it is heavy with water.

Programme 03 · Wool

Wool

A full bath with a short agitation and a cold rinse, kept below the temperature where scales lock together and felting begins. The spin is held to four hundred revolutions so the knit keeps its shape.

Programme 04 · Silk

Silk

Neutral water, no optical brighteners, and a rhythm slow enough that the lustre on the face of the cloth is not rubbed dull. The load is lifted gently so the wet weight never pulls on a seam.

Programme 05 · Cotton

Cotton

Sixty degrees for sturdy working cloth: aprons, canvas covers, undyed linings. This is the only programme that runs hot, and it is reserved for pieces whose colour was fixed and proven in the studio first.

Note 01

Detergents

Only neutral soap with a pH between seven and eight is kept in the wash house, dosed by weight rather than by capful. Nothing with enzymes, optical brighteners or fragrances ever enters the bath, because those additives keep working long after the rinse.

Every batch is recorded in the log: programme, temperature, dose and the pieces that went in, so a result can be repeated years later when a conservator is asked why a textile looks the way it does.

Note 02

Drying

Nothing is tumble dried. Pieces are rolled in clean towelling to take the weight of the water out, then laid flat on a mesh table or hung from a padded bar so the wet fibres are never asked to carry their own load.

The room is held at eighteen degrees with steady air movement, and a piece is turned every few hours until the last damp patch at a seam has gone.

Note 03

Storage

Clean textiles rest in acid free boxes with tissue in every fold, never in plastic, and never under a fold sharper than the fibre can take. Flat pieces are stored flat; anything long is rolled around a wide tube.

The store is kept dark, cool and quiet, with a card in each box listing the programme it was washed on and the date, which is the last entry anyone should ever need to add.
