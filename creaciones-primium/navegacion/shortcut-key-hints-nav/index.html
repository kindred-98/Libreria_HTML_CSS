Sheet 01 of 06 · planning memorandum

Third quarter plan
Meridian Suite

Six sheets, six keys. Every destination in this memorandum sits one digit away from the number row, so a reviewer can walk the whole document without ever touching the pointer. The chips along the top are live: press the key and the sheet lights where it lands.

Prepared by the operations desk · circulated to nine people · revision four

Sheet 02 · executive summary

What the quarter has to deliver

Three commitments carry the quarter. The document engine must open a two hundred page file in under two seconds on the oldest machine still in daily service, the review workflow must survive a dropped connection without losing a comment, and the export presets have to be honest about what they change.

Everything else is negotiable. If a feature cannot be measured against one of those three sentences, it waits for the next quarter rather than crowding the release.

Sheet 03 · figures and tables

Numbers that survived review

Opening time fell from eleven seconds to just under two on the reference machine, and the slowest figure in the new build is the first paint of a document with tracked changes, which sits at one point four seconds and is expected to improve once the diff cache lands.

Sheet 04 · team and owners

Who holds which promise

Nine people, three promises, one rule: the owner of a promise is the person who has to say no to the other eight. That is why the desk is small and why each sheet on this page names a single person rather than a committee.

Reviewers come and go across the quarter. The operations desk keeps a seat at the top of every thread, and the person who reads the most comments is not necessarily the person who writes the most.

Sheet 05 · schedule

Six weeks, two gates

The quarter is cut in two halves with a gate at the midpoint. The first half is the engine: cache, diff, paint. The second half is the workflow: presence, comments, and the export presets that have been promised to three different customers in three different words.

If the gate is missed, the second half is not compressed. It is deferred, loudly, in writing, at the top of this page. Nobody has ever regretted a written delay; everybody has regretted a silent one.

Sheet 06 · glossary

Words this office uses precisely

Gate means a written checkpoint with a named owner, not a status meeting. Sheet means one of the six numbered destinations in the bar at the top, which is also the unit of navigation for the whole document.

Key means the printed chip that shows the shortcut, and pressing it must do the same thing as clicking the destination beside it. A chip that lies is worse than no chip at all, so every one of them is wired to a real jump.
