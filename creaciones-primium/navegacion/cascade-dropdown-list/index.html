Molino & Co · Grain and pulse merchant since 1948

Two counters, one scoop

Everything behind the counter hangs from two menus. Open Grains and its two lists fall open in sequence, each one stepping out to the right; Service answers with a flat list of three. Escape puts the lid back on and hands focus to the button you started from.

Bin 01

Wheat berries

Hard red wheat from the eastern fields, cleaned on a disc separator and never heat treated, so the germ is still alive when it reaches your kitchen. Sold from the scoop, five kilos for a household and twenty-five for a bakery.

Ask for a handful to inspect: plump berries with a visible crease and no pinholes mean the weevils never found the sack.

Bin 02

Spelt and rye

Spelt is hulled on a slow rubber mill to keep the grain whole, while the rye arrives damp from the field and is dried gently before it is stored. Both keep their flavour best when they are cracked the same week you use them.

Rye is the darkest grain on the counter and the one that behaves least like wheat: it needs a wetter dough and a longer rest.

Bin 03

Barley and oats

Pearl barley is polished in short runs so the starch stays sealed inside each grain, and the oats are rolled the morning after they are dried. Both sacks are turned weekly because they settle and pack at the bottom.

Bin 04

Lentils

Three kinds rest here: small brown lentils that hold their shape, red split lentils that fall apart into soup, and a green variety from the hill terraces that keeps a firm bite after forty minutes of simmering.

They are graded on a screen table twice a week, which is why a scoop from one day can look slightly different from a scoop taken on Friday.

Bin 05

Chickpeas

The large kabuli chickpeas are sorted by diameter, so a kilo cooks evenly instead of leaving hard centres among soft ones. Soak them overnight in plenty of water with a pinch of salt and the skins stay intact.

Counter 06

Milling on site

The stone mill behind the counter turns at low speed, so the flour leaves warm rather than hot and keeps the germ mixed through it. Wholemeal is ready in minutes; pastry flour is sifted twice through a fine bolt.

Tell the miller what you are baking and the gap between the stones is set for you, from coarse semolina to a flour fine enough for sponge cake.

Counter 07

Bulk orders

Orders above a hundred kilos are milled to a date you choose rather than kept in a warehouse, and the mill runs an extra shift on Thursdays for bakery deliveries. A pallet arrives on the same crate it left in.

Standing orders can be paused for a month without losing the price agreed at the start of the season.

Counter 08

Visit the shop

The shop sits at the end of the covered market, under the iron roof, with the scales by the window and the mill in the back room. Bring your own sack and it is weighed empty before anything goes in.

Tuesday to Saturday, nine in the morning until six. On Mondays the sacks are emptied, swept and refilled.
