A corner shop
that keeps a list

Six shelves, a chest freezer and a notebook. The categories above open into their own lists, and the basket on the right keeps exactly what you put in it: change a quantity and the total moves with it.

The shop has been on this corner long enough to know who takes two loaves

Shelf 01 · fruit and vegetables

Six crates, no storage

Everything on this shelf came in a crate this morning and will be gone by Friday, which is why there is no second crate underneath and no plan B in the back. The shopkeeper buys what the market sells well, and the worst of it is never offered to a customer who did not ask for it by name.

Tomatoes are the only thing weighed in front of you, because they are the only thing that cannot be stacked honestly. Everything else is counted, and a shop that counts things out loud is a shop you can check.

Shelf 02 · pantry

Jars, tins and one stone mill

The pantry is the part of the shop that has not changed since the shopkeeper's father ran it, apart from the labels, which were reprinted twice and still have a date on them so you can see how old the tin really is.

Flour comes from a mill eleven miles away and arrives in paper sacks, because paper keeps it dry and lets the shopkeeper feel whether the sack has been somewhere warm. A damp sack goes back.

Shelf 03 · frozen

Minus twenty two, all year

The chest freezer holds two landings a week, a fortnight of peas and a quantity of ice cream nobody buys except on a hot day. The temperature is written on a card taped to the lid and the card is changed every morning, which is the only reliable temperature log in the building.

Ready meals are stacked by best before, oldest at the front. A shop that puts the newest at eye level is a shop that is trying to throw something away.

Shelf 04 · drinks

Crates by the door, and a tap

Drinks arrive in crates and stay in them, stacked by the door where the light is worst and the floor is strongest. The tap at the end of the counter sells three things and is cleaned at six every evening whether it was busy or not.

Returnable bottles are worth a penny each and the shopkeeper counts them in front of you, because an argument about a crate of empties is not an argument any customer should have to win alone.

Shelf 05 · bakery

Out of the oven at half six

The oven is behind the counter and it is on a timer, so bread comes out at half past six and again at one, and the smell reaches the bus stop early enough that people arrive before the bread does.

Two loaves is the usual order and one is the usual correction, because the first loaf is still warm and the second one is what you actually eat on the way home.

Shelf 06 · offers

Two for one, and nothing else

The shop runs a single offer at a time because a shop with six offers has none. This week it is apples, next week it will be whatever arrived by the crate and cannot be sold at a full price before Sunday.

Everything on offer is marked with a card the shopkeeper writes by hand, and the date it was written is on the card. An offer that has been up since a previous month is not an offer.
