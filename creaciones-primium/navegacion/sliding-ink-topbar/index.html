01 Studio

Drawings that
hold the light

Vantek is a nine-person practice working between Lisbon and Porto. We build with lime plaster, cork and slow concrete, and we draw every joint at full size before anyone orders a sheet of glass.

The studio occupies a former printworks on Rua da Boavista. Daylight comes from three north sawteeth, the model shelves line the south wall, and the ink on this page behaves the way our underline behaves: it settles, it does not splash.

02 Projects

Selected works

Each commission starts with a survey of what is already standing. We keep the walls that carry memory, remove the ones that only carry dust, and let the new structure announce itself with a single clean line.

  1. 01 Casa Marés Cascais · House · 2024 A courtyard house folded around two existing fig trees, with lime-washed walls that take the sea salt without complaint.
  2. 02 Printworks Lofts Lisbon · Conversion · 2023 The building you are reading this page in. Steel trusses left raw, new floors hung from the ridge, ink stains preserved as floor finish.
  3. 03 Serra Reading Room Guarda · Civic · 2022 A granite library with a cork roof, sized so that every seat sits within four metres of a window.
  4. 04 Terminal Two Canopy Porto · Infrastructure · 2021 Eleven hundred tonnes of white steel folded into a single 46-metre span, drained through the columns themselves.

03 Method

Four passes, no shortcuts

We work in four passes and we do not start the next one until the previous has been printed, pinned to the wall and lived with for a week. The underline in the bar above follows the same rule: it arrives once, smoothly, and stays put.

  1. 01

    Survey

    We measure what exists by hand, photograph it at three times of day and write a one-page brief that the client has to agree to before a single line is drawn.

  2. 02

    Massing

    Three cardboard options at 1:200, one afternoon each. Two are rejected in the room, the third survives and becomes the model we build at 1:50.

  3. 03

    Detail

    Every junction is drawn at 1:1 in ink. Where the drawing gets awkward, the construction usually gets awkward too, so we go back and change the idea.

  4. 04

    Handover

    We stay on site until the last sealant bead. The final document is a maintenance diary written for the person who will own the building in twenty years.
