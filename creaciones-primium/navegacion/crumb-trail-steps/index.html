01 / Archive

Boxes one to six, north stack

The investigation begins where every investigation begins: with a shelf. Four hundred and twelve entries, each typed on a card, each card carrying the box, the shelf and the initials of whoever last pulled it out. Nothing on the card is ever erased; corrections are struck through in pencil and initialled.

Access is granted for the room only. A slip is filled in with two signatures, the box is opened on the table under the lamp, and the slip is filed in the empty slot the box leaves behind until it returns. That empty slot is how the room knows something is out.

02 / Evidence

One hundred and eighty-seven sealed items

Physical items are logged the hour they arrive and sealed in the presence of a second person who signs the same line further down the page. The seal number is written twice: once on the bag and once in the register, in different pens, so neither can be rewritten alone.

Transfers between rooms are photographed under the same lamp at the same distance every time, which turns out to matter more than any single photograph: a difference in lighting would otherwise look like a difference in the object. Seals have held since 1974.

03 / Interviews

Thirty-one voices on twenty-eight transcripts

Every interview was recorded twice, once on reel and once on cassette, and the two were never mixed: if a word is unclear on one, the other settles it. Witnesses are named in the register but not in the transcript until they have read and initialled the page themselves.

Three transcripts remain with the typist because two speakers talk over each other in the same three minutes of tape. Those reels stay in vault B until someone sits with both channels and writes down what each of them actually said.

04 / Analysis

Fourteen threads, three contradictions

Threads are drawn on the board with string and pinned at both ends: a date, a name, a place. When two threads claim the same hour for two different rooms, both stay up and a red card is pinned between them until somebody proves one of them wrong.

Cross-referencing runs against the index rather than against memory, so an entry that has not been opened in twenty years still answers when it is asked. Three contradictions remain on the board; two of them are the same four-day gap seen from opposite ends.

05 / Report

Sixty-four pages, second review

The report cites two hundred and twelve of the four hundred and twelve index entries; the rest are background that shaped the questions without answering them. Every citation points to a box, a shelf and a card, so a reader can walk to the north stack and check it in an afternoon.

The second review is read by someone who has not worked on the file, underlined in blue and returned with a list. Nothing is removed at this stage — sentences that survive the blue pencil are the ones that finally go to print with the file number on the cover.
