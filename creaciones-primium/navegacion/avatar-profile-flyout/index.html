Section 01 · accounts

Two accounts and one habit

The current account pays nothing and costs nothing, which is deliberate: it is the account people forget about, and the branch would rather it stay boring. The savings account is where the habit lives, because a standing order on the same day each month does more for a household than any rate the bank could invent.

Both are read from the same ledger. A transfer between them is not two operations, it is one, and if one side fails the other does not happen, which is a promise the old system could not keep and the new one can.

Section 02 · cards

One card, frozen twice

Card 4417 has been frozen twice this year, once by the customer and once by the bank. The bank's freeze lasted eleven minutes and stopped a payment that turned out to be legitimate, which the branch apologised for in writing and paid for with the fee.

Every card action is confirmed twice: once in the app and once on the card reader, because a person who has been phoned by a convincing stranger learns the lesson once and keeps it.

Section 03 · transfers

Money that arrives before the notice

A transfer made before four in the afternoon lands the same evening; one made after four lands the next working morning, and the app says which before you press anything rather than after. Customers who know that rule stop calling the branch, which was the point of writing it down.

Standing orders are the quiet spine of the service. There are eleven thousand of them across the branch, and not one of them has ever been the reason for a complaint that ended in a court.

Section 04 · investments

A savings fund, carefully

The fund holds what the branch describes as a savings fund and what customers describe as the thing they can see the value of. It is not advice and it is not a product with a marketing department; it is a small, dull, transparent pot that has never once been exciting and has never once been empty.

Statements come out every quarter, in plain language, with the fees written as a number and not as a range. A range is a way of not telling somebody something.

Section 05 · security

Two people, one account

Joint accounts carry the same rules as single ones, with one addition: neither holder can be quietly removed, and the branch will not accept a request over the telephone without both of them in the room or on the same call, with a reference number read out at the start and the end.

Every sign-in is listed with a place and a time, and the list is kept for two years, because a customer who can read what happened to their account will not need to be persuaded to be careful with it.

Section 06 · support

A person, and a waiting time

The branch publishes its waiting time because a customer who is told nine minutes will usually wait nine minutes, and a customer who is told nothing will assume the worst. The queue is one number long and it is answered by people who have worked here for a median of six years.

Everything the branch can answer it answers, and the only thing it cannot answer is when a transfer will arrive, because that depends on the other bank's afternoon cut-off and no amount of goodwill moves that.
