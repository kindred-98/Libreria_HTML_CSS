Bench 01 · Standard D638 · Type V dogbone

Tensile rig

The coupon is gripped at both ends and pulled at one millimetre per minute until necking begins. Strain is read from an extensometer clamped to the gauge length, so the machine records force rather than crosshead travel.

Breaks have to happen inside the marked gauge to count. A slip at the jaws or a fracture at the shoulder sends the sample back to the rack with a red tag, because those numbers describe the fixture instead of the material.

Bench 02 · Standard D746 · 200 cycles

Thermal cycle

Samples ride between minus forty and eighty degrees Celsius in two minute legs, with a short plateau at each end so the core of the material reaches the set point instead of only the surface.

After two hundred cycles the coupon is inspected for crazing under raking light and then bent around a mandrel. Micro-cracks that only appear under magnification still fail the batch, since they are the ones that grow in service.

Bench 03 · Standard B117 · 5 % brine

Salt fog

A five per cent brine is atomised into a closed cabinet held at thirty-five degrees, producing a fine acid-free mist that settles on the panel at one and a half millilitres per hour per eighty square centimetres.

Ratings are taken every two hundred and forty hours: percentage of surface rust, blister size under a ten times lens, and whether the scribe line has crept under the film. The cabinet is opened only at the end of a run.

Bench 04 · Standard D2486 · Gloss 60°

Optical scan

The scan head travels the panel at constant speed while a laser line is projected across it. Height is sampled two thousand times per millimetre, which is enough to resolve a scratch a fingertip would never notice.

The output is a roughness map rather than a single number, so a hazy halo around a polished edge shows up as a ring of raised values. Files stay with the coupon for the life of the batch.

Log 01

Calibration log

Every bench is referenced against certified blocks on the first Monday of the month. The tensile rig runs a steel dummy of known modulus, the thermal cabinet reads a traceable probe at three points, and the salt cabinet is weighed before and after each collection period.

Deviations greater than one per cent freeze the bench until an engineer signs the sheet. Results produced between the failure and the correction are voided automatically and the affected coupons return to the rack.

Log 02

Specimen archive

Tested coupons are labelled on one end with batch, bench and date, then filed upright in shallow trays. Fracture faces are protected with a paper sleeve so a later audit can still read the origin of the break.

Trays stay five years in the dry room. Anything older is shredded, except for the reference set kept to compare a new polymer against its previous generation.

Log 03

Safety notes

The tensile rig carries enough stored energy to throw a broken coupon across the room, so the guard stays down for the whole run and nobody stands in line with the grips. The cabinet door opens only after the mist has settled.

Eye protection is required at the grinding station and whenever a panel is cut for the archive. Brine is drained into the neutralising tank, never into the sink.
