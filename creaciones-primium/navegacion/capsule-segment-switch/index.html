Larkspur LX · Device settings

Four groups, one capsule

The recorder keeps its controls in four drawers. The glass capsule glides to the drawer you pick, the panel beneath it swaps for real switches, and the arrow keys walk through the groups without leaving the keyboard.

Group 01 · Display

Screen and brightness

The panel runs at ninety hertz and drops to one when the device sits still, which is most of a recording day. Automatic brightness reads the small sensor above the earpiece rather than the front camera.

Group 02 · Audio

Monitoring and output

Line out is fixed at unity so the field mixer keeps control of level; the headphone amp has its own trim. Spatial processing is disabled while a track is armed, because it adds latency a take cannot afford.

Group 03 · Power

Battery and charging

Adaptive power save throttles the screen first and the wireless radio second, never the converters. Charging pauses at eighty per cent when the device reports a warm case, then resumes overnight.

Settings · System

Firmware and build

The device ships with firmware 4.2.1 and updates over the charging dock, never over the air during a take. A failed update rolls back to the previous slot automatically and reports the fault the next time the dock is opened.

Build numbers are printed inside the battery door so a unit can be identified without power, which matters when a batch of capsules has to be traced back to a week of production.

Settings · Storage

Card and offload

Recordings are written twice, to the card and to internal flash, for the first minute of every take. After that the card alone carries the file while the mirror is cleared to keep write latency low.

Offload happens by cable at full speed. Wireless transfer is available but it copies a proxy first, so the original file never leaves the device while the battery is below twenty per cent.

Settings · Service

Repair and parts

The back panel is held by four standard screws and the connector board lifts out without heat. Replacement capsules pair to the host on first contact, so a swapped part needs no visit to a service centre.

Spare batteries stay available for seven years from the end of production. Recalibration after a repair is a two minute routine that runs from the same drawer you are reading now.
