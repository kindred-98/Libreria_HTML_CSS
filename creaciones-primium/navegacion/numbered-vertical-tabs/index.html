Six scripts that were never broadcast

Indexed by number, read by ear. Every sheet in this drawer is a complete script: a running time, a cast of voices, and the notes the continuity clerk left in the margin before the tapes were wiped.

Thread running1958—83

Sheet 01/Serie B/Twelve chapters

La Última Bodegón

A nightly inn in a port town, written for eleven voices and a rain machine. The serial was cancelled after four chapters when the sponsor found a cheaper drama in the same slot.

Year
1958
Running time
28 min per chapter
Cast
11 voices
Status
Banned at chapter 5

Rain is the only sound effect the script asks for, and it is asked for in every single chapter, which is the sort of obsession that gets a serial noticed by the wrong person. The innkeeper counts bottles at the top of the hour and the counting is the theme: by the last chapter he is counting the days since his daughter left, and the audience only understands that in the final line.

The continuity clerk noted that the cast could not hold the pause in chapter nine and that the network solved it by adding a commercial. Both decisions are still in the drawer, filed with the log sheets, and the tape of chapter nine does exist for eleven seconds before the interference.

  1. Ch. 01Counting bottles28:00
  2. Ch. 05The sponsor reads the log27:40
  3. Ch. 09Rain, eleven seconds00:11
  4. Ch. 12Never recorded—

Sheet 02/Serie A/Nine chapters

Cuarenta Grados

A heatwave serial written in one week by a telephone operator on her night shift. Broadcast once, then pulled from the archive for twenty-two years.

Year
1963
Running time
22 min per chapter
Cast
6 voices
Status
Broadcast, then withdrawn

The whole plot is told through a temperature: the power fails in the block where the narrator lives, the ice melts, the water rises, and the temperature is read aloud on a thermometer she keeps on the shelf. The writing is almost all numbers, and the producer argued for an hour that the audience would not stay.

He was right, and the audience of four thousand who did stay wrote to the station for a decade afterwards. The scripts were reissued in 1985 and are the only reason the author's name survives at all; the copy in this drawer has her corrections pencilled in the margins of chapter six, where she changes a single temperature and the whole ending follows.

  1. Ch. 01Thirty-one degrees22:10
  2. Ch. 04The block goes dark21:50
  3. Ch. 06One number changed22:30
  4. Ch. 09Forty, finally23:15

Sheet 03/Serie C/Seven chapters

El Sonido Del Pozo

A well, a rope, and a village that will not drill. Recorded in a cistern with a live reverb that the engineers spent a month trying to remove.

Year
1967
Running time
31 min per chapter
Cast
9 voices
Status
Unfinished at chapter 7

The script asks for a specific silence: after the rope goes down, a gap of four seconds in which nothing is said and nothing is played. In the studio those four seconds were the most valuable thing in the drawer, because the room itself answered with water and stone and the audience heard a village instead of a booth.

Chapter seven stops mid-sentence on the tape. The writer died in the winter of 1968 with the outline for eight more chapters in her desk, and the archive kept the outline folded into the back cover of this script, where it still is.

  1. Ch. 01The rope goes down31:00
  2. Ch. 03Four seconds of nothing30:20
  3. Ch. 05The village denies it30:55
  4. Ch. 07Stops mid-sentence04:12

Sheet 04/Serie B/Ten chapters

Niebla En La Avenida

A city serial in which the fog does most of the work. The sound department built a fog horn and then, by accident, the plot.

Year
1971
Running time
26 min per chapter
Cast
14 voices
Status
Full run, reissued 1994

Every character in this serial lies, and the audience cannot tell who is lying until chapter six, when the fog horn answers a question that was asked in chapter one. The writers built the whole thing backwards from that single sound, and the continuity sheet for the series is a list of questions with the chapter in which each one is answered.

It is the only complete script in the drawer with every chapter present, and it is here as a control: the index points at it because a drawer of six sheets needs one that runs to the end, so the reader can hear where the tape is going before they start.

  1. Ch. 01The question26:00
  2. Ch. 03Two hundred horns25:30
  3. Ch. 06The answer26:40
  4. Ch. 10Silence, on purpose27:05

Sheet 05/Serie D/Eight chapters

Dos Hermanas

Two sisters, one microphone, one long performance. The only script in the drawer written to be read by the same actress on both sides.

Year
1976
Running time
29 min per chapter
Cast
1 voice, 2 characters
Status
Broadcast, badly timed

The sisters argue about a house for eight chapters and never once raise their voices, which is harder to write and much harder to perform. The only cue for who is speaking is a change in how the name of the house is said, and the actress was given the whole rehearsal budget the network could not spare for anything else.

It went out at nine in the evening on a night of heavy rain, sat in a slot meant for a cooking programme, and drew complaints from a third of the letters. The other two thirds asked for the repeat. Both halves of that response are stapled to the front of the script.

  1. Ch. 01The name of the house29:00
  2. Ch. 04Same voice, other sister28:20
  3. Ch. 07The roof, finally30:10
  4. Ch. 08Letters from the audience29:45

Sheet 06/Serie A/Six chapters

Cierre De Emisión

A closing-time serial, six chapters, the last thing the station broadcast before midnight moved to continuous music.

Year
1983
Running time
24 min per chapter
Cast
7 voices
Status
Last drama ever aired

The premise is a bar that empties at closing time and never quite empties, because each chapter loses one customer and gains one narrator. By the last chapter the only voice left is the one listing the empty tables, and the closing music begins underneath the list rather than after it.

There is no resolution because the station had none to give: the tapes were wiped the following spring to make room for a format. The script survived as a photocopy, and the photocopy is the reason this drawer is still catalogued rather than recycled.

  1. Ch. 01One customer leaves24:00
  2. Ch. 03The list begins23:30
  3. Ch. 05Nobody at table nine24:20
  4. Ch. 06Music under the list25:00
