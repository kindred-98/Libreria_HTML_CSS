What the party expects to lift

One sealed strongbox, listed in the only surviving hand as containing plate and nothing else. The hand disagrees with itself about the plate.

Two sealed bottles, tied with cord to the handles, for the reason every party ties bottles to handles.

A letter, unopened, in a language nobody on the sheet can read. It has been read twice and returned twice.

Whatever else. The sheet stops here, which after three pages of names and numbers is doing a great deal of work.

Mark 01 · 0 leagues · low tide

Cueva del Alabado

Everything starts in a cove nobody uses, at the bottom of a cliff of soft yellow rock that crumbles if you lean on it. The first cross is pricked into a flat slab above the tide line, about four feet above the ground, which tells you the person who made this sheet was not worried about weather and was very worried about being seen.

From the cove the route goes inland in a straight line for six hundred paces, which is the single most suspicious thing about it, and then turns. Everything after this point is a series of turns. The first night is always spent here, on the slab, because the walk back to the cove in the dark is a walk of about twenty minutes that takes people three hours.

Mark 02 · 1 league · dry at any tide

Puente de Sal

A natural arch of rock the colour of a dry bone, with a cut on the underside at about head height. The cut is old, the arch is older, and the sheet records both without explaining, which is either discipline or a man who did not want to explain it to whoever came next.

Cross at midday. The rock holds heat, the floor of the arch is covered in a hard pan of salt that looks like ice and is not, and the light through the gap at the correct hour of the day is bright enough to hurt. Everybody does it at noon. Nobody enjoys it and nobody does it at any other time.

Mark 03 · 2 leagues · wall in ruins

Horno Viejo

The burnt kiln of a lime works that closed before anybody living was born, with its lime ovens still standing and one of them still a recognisable oven. The third cross is on the door jamb, drawn in charcoal and gone soft at the edges from rain, which means the sheet was made within living memory of the last working season.

There is fresh water here and the sheet does not mention it, because the sheet is not a guide to water. Take what you need and fill up: it runs out of a spring under the second oven and tastes of stone. The floor of the kiln bay is the best place on the whole route to sleep and everybody camps there, whatever the sheet says.

Mark 04 · 3 leagues · warning

Cala del Lobo

The skull on the sheet sits here, which is the only place on it where a skull sits. Cala del Lobo is a good cove, a deep one, with a sand bottom, shelter from the west, and about a fathom of water over a shelf of very good rock that has eaten one boat that anybody remembers.

There is no marker on the ground. There is a scratch on a boulder above the high-water line, a triangle, and a line through it, and the sheet explains this once, in the margin, in a different and smaller hand: here the ground is a lie. Do not dig here. Walk past the headland and come back to it in the dark, which is the only time anybody has ever found what the sheet was talking about.

Mark 05 · 4 leagues · last cairn

Cruz de Risco

There is a real cross here, dry stone, waist high, and a flat capstone that works as a table, a seat and a windbreak depending on what you need. This is the last place the route is certain. The fifth mark is scratched on the capstone beside two other sets of scratches, all of them older than the sheet.

From the cross the last leg runs north for a quarter of a league across open ground with nothing on it, and the sheet describes the ground exactly, in careful detail, which is how you know the writer crossed it a great many times. Walk it at dusk. Walk it slowly. And when you see the brow of the hill, do not hurry, because the last hundred yards are the only part anybody can be watching.

Mark 06 · 5 leagues · chest

Cofre de la Ceja

The chest of the brow, and the reason for the name. It is below a flat slab at the top of the hill, dug in, filled with stones, and covered with the same stones so that the whole thing is a lump on a hillside that looks exactly like a hundred other lumps. The last cross is not pricked on the ground. It is a small cairn with one stone turned over, and you are meant to leave it that way when you go back.

Everything the sheet promised is there, in the order it promised it, except the strongbox, which is lighter than the list says by about nine kilograms. There is a note inside in the same small hand as the margin at Cala del Lobo, and it is two words long. The party has argued about what the two words mean for forty years and has not dug again.
