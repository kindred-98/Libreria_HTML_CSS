9 km de cresta

Reading the profile

Solid brown is the walking line. The lighter brown laid over it is the part of the walk already described in the log below.

Dark green is the near slope and pale green is the ridge behind it. They are one mountain drawn twice, because that is the only way a flat drawing can show a slope.

The blue cursor reads the altitude at the milestone under the reader. It climbs or drops with the profile and never floats above the line.

The numbers at the left are five hundred metres apart, and they are the only scale on the whole sheet.

Milestone 01 · 760 m · km 0.0

Pie de la Cresta

Where the track from the village gives up and joins the ridge line, at a gap between two shoulders of the same height. A surveyor put a stone here in 1911, somebody put a better stone on top of it in 1954, and both are still there, which is the usual arrangement in this district and the reason the route can be described without a single photograph.

From the foot of the ridge the profile climbs six hundred and forty metres over three and a half kilometres, and every part of that climb is visible from the first stone, laid out in front of you the whole way. The track is marked at intervals by stones that somebody keeps painting, at intervals that have never been published, and the first milestone is not marked on the ground at all because the ground there is a field.

Milestone 02 · 940 m · km 1.8

Horno de la Vera

A charcoal kiln, one room and a chimney, abandoned in the nineteen-forties and used as a landmark by everybody who has ever needed to say where they were. It stands two hundred metres below the ridge line and slightly off it, and from the second milestone you can see the whole climb above you at once, which is either encouraging or not, depending on the day.

The climb from the foot to the second milestone is the longest gentle gradient in the district: a hundred and eighty metres over a kilometre and a half, never steep, never switchbacked, and it is the part of the route where people talk and the part where most of them make the mistake of drinking too little. The stone is a genuine milestone, set upright in a wall that used to divide two charcoal yards.

Milestone 03 · 1 400 m · km 3.6

Collado de las Zorras

The high point of the whole profile, and the reason the profile is drawn in this particular shape. It is a bare saddle of grey rock between a shoulder to the north and the top of the ridge to the south, and the name is a joke that stopped being a joke about ninety years ago and is now simply the name.

There are two cairns on it facing each other across the gap, and the marked stone is the third. From here the whole route lies in front of you and the profile lies on the table, and the two are almost exactly the same shape: three and a half kilometres of climbing, then a long clean descent with nothing on it worth stopping for. Walkers who get to the col in the first hour of the day and walkers who get there in the fourth arrive at exactly the same place.

Milestone 04 · 960 m · km 5.1

Hito de la Encina

A solitary oak, four hundred years old by any estimate anybody is willing to defend, standing in a fold of ground four hundred metres below the ridge. The milestone is a hundred metres away and a hundred and forty metres above it, on a shoulder of rock, and from the stone you cannot see the tree at all. You can see it perfectly well from the col, which is where everybody decides to look for it.

A wall has been built around the oak four times, most recently in 1971, and the wall is now worth less than the tree in every sense that can be measured. The descent from the col to the second milestone is the fastest in the district, four hundred and forty metres in a kilometre and a half, and everybody walks it too fast. That is the whole practical content of this entry and the rest is a tree.

Milestone 05 · 800 m · km 7.4

Vado del Fresno

The last open ground, and a seasonal ford with a line of stepping stones across it that is reliable from about June to September and lethal-looking at any other time. The milestone is set in the wall of a sheep shelter because the obvious place for a milestone is under water for a third of the year, which is a decision that has been made on this mountain more than once.

An ash tree on the far bank gave the place its name and gave the profile its one moment of interest: the ground dips, the ford fills, and the line on the drawing goes almost flat for two hundred metres before resuming its descent. Everybody stops at the ford, not because of the water but because it is the only place in the last three kilometres where the ridge is wide enough to stop on.

Milestone 06 · 740 m · km 9.0

Vaguada del Molino

Where the profile ends: a stream in a gully, a mill that stopped grinding in 1968, and a flat piece of ground about the size of a tennis court with a track leaving it towards the village. The ridge has been walking for nine kilometres and the last three hundred metres of the drawing are simply the mountain getting out of the way.

The descent from the ford is short, gentle and entirely without incident, which is the correct way for a route to end and is why this log spends so much of itself on the middle of it. Walk the profile from the left. The cursor climbs to each milestone in turn, the laid part of the line grows, and at the sixth one the whole nine kilometres have been walked and the cursor is back down near the floor of the axis.
