Nothing here grows on purpose

Words by Marisol Ferrante · Photographs by Ines Brauer · 24 min read

The first thing you notice in Cal d’Ombra is that nobody is in a hurry, and the second is that everything is late. The bakery opens when the ovens decide it does. The bus leaves when the driver has finished his coffee. A week here is not a unit of time but a length of road, and the road only goes as far as the next bend.

So the town made a decision that sounded like a joke. The council closed the visitor office and opened a tool library instead. Hammers, welders, a wood lathe, three sewing machines and a kiln that anybody on the register could book for an hour at a time. Nobody called it an economy. Everybody started calling it a Tuesday.

By the second winter the exchange had settled into something with its own arithmetic. Forty hours of lathe time bought a week of bread. An hour of childcare covered an hour of boiler repair. Nobody was getting rich and nobody was keeping score, which is exactly the point the mayor kept making to anyone from the regional paper who came looking for a trend.

We stopped asking what the valley could produce and started asking what it already had, sitting in a cupboard, waiting for a Tuesday.

Ilaria Bentivoglio, mayor of Cal d’Ombra
