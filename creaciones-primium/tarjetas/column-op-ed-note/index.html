The quiet cost of a cheaper grid

By N. Okonkwo · 640 words · draft 3

Every winter the city rewires a third of its supply and bills the difference to a committee that meets once.

The arithmetic looks clean on the wall chart: fewer losses, cheaper wins, a grid that finally matches the demand curve. What the chart leaves out is the crew that climbs the pole at four in the morning to find the joint that never seated.

Marking in the margin: the saving is real but it is smaller than advertised, and the repair bill lands two winters later, on a different budget line, in a different ward.

A cheaper grid is not a cheaper grid. It is the same grid, deferred, with the interest paid by somebody who did not sign the contract.
