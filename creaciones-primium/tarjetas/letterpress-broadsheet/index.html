A Map of the Last Bookbinders

Six workshops remain inside the ring road. Each keeps a different glue, a different smell and a different hour to pull the shutter down.

The composing room works in reverse. Letters are lifted from the case backwards, locked into the chase, and only then does the sentence appear on the proofing stone, correct and upside down to everyone except the monotype man.

He has held the same stool since the era of hot metal, and he can tell a misrun by its sound before the sheet reaches his hand.

Ink arrives at midnight in steel drums, mixed to a colour nobody writes down. The formula lives in a stained notebook that the foreman keeps under the register.

By four the piles are stacked by the loading bay, still warm, each one carrying a smell the press men call, without irony, the perfume of the district.
